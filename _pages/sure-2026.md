### Demo 1
In this clip, I asked Cosmos-Reason2-2B 5 questions — one from each category in our benchmark:

* **Basic Understanding:** Is there a pedestrian visible in the crosswalk during the clip?
* **Attribution:** How many vehicles are stopped at the intersection at the start?
* **Event Reasoning:** Does any car make a right turn during the clip?
* **Reverse Reasoning:** Did the red pickup truck finish driving through the intersection before the pedestrians finished crossing the crosswalk?
* **Counterfactual Inference:** What if the red pickup truck had turned right instead of continuing straight through the intersection? Would any pedestrians have been at risk?

### Demo 2
In this demo, I tested Cosmos-Reason2-2B on a dashcam rear-end collision. As the conversation progresses, the model displays severe reasoning breakdowns:

* **Hallucination:** Fabricates an uninvolved crash between a white SUV and a black car that appears to be in the oncoming lane on the other side of the median.
* **Inverted Physical Reasoning:** States that the gap between vehicles shrinks because the lead sedan accelerates.
* **Denial of Ground Truth:** Insists the ego-vehicle maintains a safe distance and is not part of any collision.
* **Contradiction:** Reverses its claim to admit the ego-vehicle collides when prompted directly, before immediately reverting back to its initial hallucinated narrative.
