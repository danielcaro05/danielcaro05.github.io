As part of the Summer Undergraduate Research Experience (SURE) 2026 at University of Michigan-Dearborn, we evaluated causal and counterfactual reasoning in Vision-Language Models for urban traffic. This poster summarizes our work and will be presented for SURE 2026.

**Project summary:** Vision-Language Models are increasingly used in autonomous driving, but evaluating causal reasoning remains hard. We built a standardized multiple-choice benchmark on the Urban Dynamics Video QA (UDVideoQA) dataset (300 questions across 30 clips, 5 reasoning categories) and introduced a two-stage *Analyze-then-Generate* prompting strategy to automatically generate counterfactual ("what if") question-answer pairs. We evaluated six VLMs, focusing on edge-deployable models like **Cosmos-Reason2-2B**, and found Qwen2.5-VL-7B strongest overall, while even the best models fell significantly short of what is required for real-world autonomous driving.
